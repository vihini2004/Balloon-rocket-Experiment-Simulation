    What the simulator computes

    The engine is the v3 balloon-rocket model, derived step by step in the physics guide. It solves three equations for position, speed and the air left in the balloon:

    • Rubber: a thin spherical membrane sets the pressure (Mooney–Rivlin, Gent, or your measured deflation curve).
    • Air inside: ideal gas at room temperature, or cooling adiabatically as it expands.
    • Jet: quasi-steady, subsonic, isentropic nozzle flow, corrected by the discharge and velocity coefficients. Thrust is ṁ × jet speed.
    • Motion: the variable-mass equation of motion with drag, buoyancy, added mass (including the recovery term of a shrinking body), Coulomb friction on the wire, slope and jet angle.

    Integration uses adaptive Dormand–Prince Runge–Kutta (rtol 10⁻⁸, maximum step 0.01 s) with exact detection of the stop and wire-end events. On nine test cases this page reproduces the Python reference model to four decimals in final distance and stop time, and within 0.04 % in peak speed.

    The fit uses Nelder–Mead least squares; its ± values come from the curvature of the fit (Jacobian at the optimum) and assume independent, equal-size measurement errors. The uncertainty analysis draws independent Gaussian samples of every input with a non-zero uncertainty, following the Monte Carlo method of GUM Supplement 1.
